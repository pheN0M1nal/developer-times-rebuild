## The Lead

A sporadic failure whispered across the logs: a tracking page that froze for a few seconds, once or twice an hour, and never while anyone was watching. Too quick to catch, too costly to ignore.

<PullQuote>Bugs hide in timing. Make time visible.</PullQuote>

## Method

<Evidence title="Instrumentation">

We added trace spans around every step between a courier's phone and the customer's screen, and widened sampling at the edges where messages entered and left the system.

</Evidence>

- Isolate the concurrency hotspots
- Record queue depth every second, not every minute
- Put the same clock on every machine before trusting any timestamp

## The Break

The traces lined up at rush hour. Couriers' phones, coming back into signal, sent their buffered pings all at once. The fan-out dutifully delivered every single one, in order, to every open page — a minute of history replayed in two seconds.

```ts
// Keep only the newest ping per courier before fanning out.
const latest = new Map<string, Ping>();
for (const ping of batch) latest.set(ping.courierId, ping);
broadcast([...latest.values()]);
```

## Findings

- The freeze was never a crash. It was a queue doing exactly what it was told.
- Coalescing pings removed the stalls and cut message volume by a factor of six.

## Closing Note

The hardest bugs are the polite ones. They do nothing wrong; they just do everything at once.
