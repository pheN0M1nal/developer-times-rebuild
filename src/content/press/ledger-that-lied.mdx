## Initial Report

The call came in at quarter-end. The finance team had closed the books, opened them again, and closed them a second time. The balance was off by an amount small enough to ignore and large enough that nobody could.

It balanced on Tuesdays. Nobody could say why.

## Evidence: The Duplicates

The first suspect was easy to find once we looked. A payment provider retried a webhook after a timeout; our handler had already paid the supplier, and paid them again. Two rows, same amount, same minute.

```ts
// Before: the handler trusted the provider to call once.
await payouts.create({ supplierId, amount });

// After: the provider's event id is the idempotency key.
await payouts.createOnce(event.id, { supplierId, amount });
```

<Evidence title="Exhibit B · The outbox">

Every call to a provider is first written to an outbox table in the same transaction as the ledger entry. A worker sends it and marks it done. If the worker dies halfway, it tries again — and the provider, given the same key, does nothing twice.

</Evidence>

## Evidence: The Tuesday Problem

The Tuesday pattern was the clever one. Our exchange rates came from a feed that refreshed once a day. Charges were captured in one currency and settled in another, usually a day later. We applied the rate at settlement. On Tuesdays — the provider's batch day — capture and settlement happened within the same rate window, so the numbers agreed.

<PullQuote>The rate is part of the evidence. Record it when the money moves, not when it lands.</PullQuote>

The fix was to store the rate, and its source, at capture. Any difference at settlement became its own ledger line: an FX gain or loss, visible and explainable, instead of a rounding ghost.

## Findings

- Duplicate payouts: none since the outbox shipped.
- The Tuesday pattern disappeared the day rates were captured with the charge.
- Ninety-two per cent of bank lines now match automatically. The rest wait in a review queue with a reason attached.

## Closing Note

Most reconciliation bugs are not arithmetic. They are stories told in the wrong order. Write the story down in the order it happened, and the numbers usually agree with you.
